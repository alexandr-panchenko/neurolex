# Launch, Integrate, and Distribute

**Where this happens:** with the human, implementation agents, and the actual adoption channel—judges, users, customer stakeholders, a community, or an agent integration environment.

**What it produces:** a product that its intended human or agent audience can actually find, access, use, and adopt, plus feedback from that use.

The route was chosen in [the initial design conversation](design-project-and-write-handoff.md). This step executes and tests it. It is not the first time to decide who the product serves, how it is distributed, or who pays. Nor is it necessarily the first deployment: working slices may already be deployed and early pilot preparation may be underway.

## Execute the chosen route

For a hackathon, make the requested demo and repository accessible, provide a short reproducible judging path, and ensure required services and inputs remain available for the judging period. Follow the actual competition requirements, rather than a generic launch checklist.

For a company integration, work through the intended sponsor and users, obtain the necessary access, demonstrate a representative pilot, and address adoption or approval barriers. A working public website does not substitute for fitting the company's actual process.

For a self-service product, verify discovery, onboarding, first value, access, and the selected payment flow. For agent-mediated distribution, test the caller's real discovery/install, authorization, invocation, result retrieval, and continuation path. A published tool description alone is not adoption.

Use only the relevant route. Do not create obligatory marketing funnels, dashboards, subscription screens, or marketplace listings for a product that does not need them.

## Verify the full access and value chain

Check that the intended participant can reach a meaningful result without hidden developer setup. Use representative permissions and data. Where human approval or spending limits matter, exercise those boundaries through both the human and agent paths that are actually supported.

Authentication, entitlements, payment providers, and accounts should already have been implemented when the working scenario required them. A late missing account is a remaining dependency to resolve—not a reason to keep all payment and access work as an end-of-project ritual.

Make the shortest useful demonstration show the outcome. Shareable artifacts, concise instructions, capability descriptions, and a clear example can support adoption when appropriate. Explain meaningful limits without requiring a prospective user to read the development documentation.

## Bring evidence back into the product

Observe real failures, abandoned paths, missing integrations, misunderstood capabilities, and qualitative reactions to results. Combine available operational evidence with direct feedback; neither substitutes for the other in every case.

Return concrete findings to [implementation and review](implement-and-review-working-product.md). If actual adoption reveals a different user, task, or distribution model, use [the redesign entry](refresh-project-for-redesign.md) instead of treating every mismatch as a marketing problem.
